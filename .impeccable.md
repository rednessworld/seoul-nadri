## Design Context

### Users
Mixed audience across three overlapping groups:
- **Barcelona locals & foodies** — young professionals, food-curious residents; may visit multiple times
- **Tourists & travelers** — discovering Korean BBQ for the first time; need reassurance and atmosphere
- **Korean expats & enthusiasts** — need authenticity signals; they know the food vocabulary

All groups arrive via Instagram, Google Maps, or word-of-mouth. The primary job-to-be-done is **deciding whether to book** — the site must answer "is this worth it?" in seconds.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Warm. Intentional. Unhurried.

Seoul Nadri sits at the intersection of a European brasserie and a Korean Hanok — refined but never cold, elegant but approachable. The brand concept is *Yeobaek-ui mi* (여백의 미) — the Korean "Beauty of Emptiness": meaning lives in what's left out, not what's piled in.

Tone of voice: A knowledgeable friend who happens to love Korean food, recommending this place with genuine warmth — not a PR brochure, not a Michelin-star pitch. Personal, generous, slightly poetic.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Visual tone**: Minimalist Editorial — premium lifestyle magazine energy, not corporate fine-dining sterility.

**References**:
- Natural textures: Hanji paper, charred wood (sumi), polished brass
- Palette anchors: Hanji Cream `#F0ECE4` / Brasserie Gold `#9A845A` / Slate Charcoal `#231F20` / Soft Greige `#D9D2C5`
- Custom serif heading font (headers_font.otf) + DM Sans for body

**Anti-reference — explicitly avoid**:
- Cold white minimalism that intimidates rather than invites
- Corporate fine-dining sterility (stark, precious, unapproachable)
- Red-and-black "Asian restaurant" clichés
- Delivery-app visual language (pill buttons, floating food cards, white backgrounds)
- Beige-and-sage Instagram brasserie clichés

**Theme**: Light only. Warm cream backgrounds, not dark moody interiors.

### Design Principles
1. **Warmth over prestige** — every design decision should feel like an invitation, not a gatekeeping. Generous whitespace, soft shadows, approachable CTAs.
2. **Restraint earns attention** — follow *Yeobaek-ui mi*: let each element breathe. One strong visual per section beats five competing ones.
3. **Authenticity over aesthetics** — the Korean language, the banchan tradition, the BBQ ritual — these cultural details are the soul of the brand. Surface them in copy, not just decoration.
4. **Mobile is primary** — the typical visitor is looking this up on their phone while walking in the Eixample. Everything must work perfectly at arm's length, one thumb.
5. **Gold is earned, not scattered** — Brasserie Gold (`#9A845A`) is the accent, not the default. Use it for one focal element per section: a rule, a CTA border, a label underline.
